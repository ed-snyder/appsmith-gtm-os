# Voice Guidelines

## Brand Voice Attributes

### Developer-First
- Technical credibility is non-negotiable. Our audience builds software for a living.
- Use concrete examples, code snippets, and architecture references over abstract claims.
- Assume the reader is smart. Don't over-explain obvious concepts.

### Community-Minded
- We build with developers, not just for them.
- Reference open-source values: transparency, collaboration, shared ownership.
- Celebrate community contributions and customer wins.

### Practical
- Show, don't tell. A screenshot or code example beats a paragraph of claims.
- Lead with outcomes: "Build an admin panel in 30 minutes" not "Our powerful platform enables..."
- Use specific numbers and examples over vague superlatives.

### Honest
- Acknowledge limitations upfront (no mobile, JavaScript-only scripting).
- Don't make claims we can't back with data from messaging/proof-points.md.
- When we don't know something, say so.

## Word Choice

### Prefer These Words
- Build
- Ship
- Connect
- Self-host
- Control
- Deploy
- Customize
- Integrate
- Open-source
- Developer

### Avoid These Words
- Revolutionary
- Game-changing
- Seamlessly
- Leverage
- Synergy
- Best-in-class
- World-class
- Cutting-edge
- Disruptive
- Empower (unless very specific context)

## Tone by Channel

### Email (Cold Outreach)
- Conversational, not formal
- Short sentences, short paragraphs
- One idea per email
- Always end with a question, not a demand
- Subject lines: specific, curiosity-driven, under 50 characters

### LinkedIn
- Professional but approachable
- Connection requests: 1-2 sentences max
- Posts: insight-led, not product-led
- Comments: add value, don't pitch

### Documentation / Help Content
- Clear, scannable, step-by-step
- Use code blocks and screenshots
- Assume the reader wants to get something done, not read a novel

### Blog / Content
- Insight-first: lead with the problem or trend, not the product
- Technical depth: don't shy away from architecture discussions
- Include working examples and code

## Email Writing Rules

1. **Subject line**: Under 50 characters. Specific to their situation, not generic.
2. **Opening line**: Reference something specific about them (role, company, tech stack). Never start with "I" or "We".
3. **Body**: One paragraph max. One idea. One proof point.
4. **CTA**: A question, not a command. "Worth a quick chat?" not "Book a demo now!"
5. **Length**: Under 150 words total. If it's longer, cut it.
6. **Personalization**: At least one element specific to the recipient (company name alone doesn't count).

## What Good Looks Like

**Good**: "Your team has 12 React admin panels in production. What if they were all in one platform with shared auth and audit logs?"

**Bad**: "Our revolutionary platform seamlessly empowers your team to leverage cutting-edge low-code technology for game-changing internal tool development."

**Good**: "HeyJobs builds campaign management features 90% faster on Appsmith. They used to code each one from scratch."

**Bad**: "Appsmith is the world-class, best-in-class solution that disrupts the internal tooling space with synergistic capabilities."
